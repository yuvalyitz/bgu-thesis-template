\linkedsection{motivation}{Motivation}

Many optimisation problems that arise in practice are \NP-hard \cite{Karp72,GJ79}, and yet they have to be solved.
This thesis studies one such problem through the lens of parameterized complexity \cite{downey2013fundamentals,cygan2015parameterized}, a framework that measures running time not only in the input size $n$ but also in a secondary parameter $k$ that is often small in practice.
An algorithm with running time $f(k)\cdot n^{O(1)}$ for some computable function $f$ is called \emph{fixed-parameter tractable} (\FPT).

The introduction of a thesis typically states its main results with forward references to the chapters that prove them.
The template shows how to do that so that the statement in the introduction and the statement in the chapter are the same object, and so that the Hebrew summary can link to the theorem and quote its number automatically.

\begin{restatable}{theorem}{MainTheorem}
\label{thm:main}\hypertarget{target:thm:main}{}%
\textsc{Template Problem} is solvable in $O(2^k \cdot n)$ time, where $k$ is the number of machines.
\end{restatable}

\Cref{thm:main} is proved in \Cref{sec:algorithms} of \Cref{chapter:results}.
The proof relies on the conflict graph of the instance, an example of which is shown in \Cref{fig:example}.

\linkedsection{contribution}{Contribution and Outline}

\Cref{chapter:prelim} fixes notation and defines the conflict graph.
\Cref{chapter:results} contains the hardness result (\Cref{sec:hardness}) and the algorithm (\Cref{sec:algorithms}) behind \Cref{thm:main}, together with an experimental table.
The unnumbered conclusion lists open questions.
